AI Applications / Information Extraction

Trade Instruction Parsing

Trade details can appear in text, screenshots and tables. Inconsistent wording and multiple instructions in a single message make the information harder to organise and verify.

I contributed to image and text processing and instruction parsing, combining Python rules, large language models and output checks to explore consistent, traceable outputs from complex inputs.

Explore the approach with an example ↗
01

Start with clear inputs

Address OCR errors, misaligned tables and mixed instructions through text cleanup, instruction splitting and field normalisation.

Image and Text Processing · Data Cleanup
02

Give rules and models distinct roles

Use Python for deterministic parsing and language models for complex meaning. Preserve existing results when merging outputs to reduce overwriting.

Rule-Based Parsing · Language Models
03

Build checks into the process

Check field formats, output structure and information sources, then analyse errors to make results easier to verify and trace.

Structure Validation · Error Analysis
Learn more: why use layered processing?+

Deterministic rules are easy to review and suit clearly formatted content; language models handle more varied wording. Layered processing requires attention to coverage, model call costs and the risk of overwriting existing results.

My focus is to define each layer's role, then check the merged structure and sources. Internal company data, code and system details are not shown here.

When recognition failsFix the input, then review the model output.Explore the approach +

OCR can confuse or omit characters and misalign tables, directly affecting field extraction. I first identify whether an issue comes from recognition, data cleanup or language understanding, then adjust that step.

When a message has multiple instructionsDefine boundaries to keep details together.Explore the approach +

Different items in one message may have different amounts, directions and dates. Establishing instruction boundaries, then checking how fields match each item, helps locate splitting and merging errors.

When outputs conflictCheck sources and preserve existing results.Explore the approach +

Rules and models may produce different outputs. Source tracking, structure checks and merge constraints help verify where information came from and whether additions or overwrites are allowed before analysing specific errors.

Machine learningML / 01

Materials Classification and Model Comparison

Comparing models using material features.

What I did
Processed features for the Matbench Glass classification task and compared random forests, support vector machines and neural networks.
How I validated it
Used cross-validation and hyperparameter optimisation, with regularisation, dropout and early stopping to control overfitting.
What I learned
Compare approaches with consistent evaluation, paying attention to data splits and generalisation.
View method details +

Used material features for classification, comparing traditional machine learning with deep neural networks. Tuned hyperparameters with Bayesian optimisation and used L2 regularisation, batch normalisation, dropout and early stopping, examining the gap between training and validation performance.

Experimental ResearchLAB / 02

ITO Transparent Conductive Films

Connecting fabrication conditions, structure and optoelectronic properties.

What I did
Studied ITO and Zn-doped ITO films to explore how deposition conditions affect film properties.
How I validated it
Combined XRD, AFM, SEM / EDX and optical transmission tests to examine structure, morphology, composition and performance.
What I learned
Investigate unexpected results and use evidence from multiple measurements to test experimental interpretations.
View experiment details +

Prepared films by pulsed laser deposition, examining deposition temperature, oxygen conditions and Zn doping. Combined optical transmission, electrical measurements and structural characterisation, and analysed cracking, adhesion and uniformity issues.

Vision and SignalsVISION / 03

Contactless Heart Rate Detection

Extracting subtle physiological signals from facial video.

What I did
Used imaging photoplethysmography (IPPG) to estimate heart rate from changes in skin colour in video.
How I built it
Used OpenCV to locate regions of interest and detect skin colour, extracting colour-channel signals over time.
What I learned
Connect image processing and signal analysis to a practical measurement problem.
View the approach +

Converted skin regions in consecutive video frames into time series, then analysed pulse-related periodic changes. Compared colour channels and examined how signal quality and recording conditions affect results.

Optoelectronic DevicesDEVICE / 04

Thin Films and Infrared Photoconductivity

From material fabrication to reading weak photoelectric signals.

What I did
Worked on and led PbSe / PbTe thin-film and photoconductivity projects, linking fabrication, material treatment and device testing.
How I built it
Used chemical bath deposition, sensitisation treatments and weak photocurrent readout circuits to test infrared response.
What I learned
Understand how materials, processing and testing together affect device performance.
View engineering details +

Examined deposition conditions and film thickness consistency. Explored device response through surface treatment, oxygen sensitisation and iodine sensitisation, using readout circuits to observe electrical signals under changing illumination.
